Building an AI application does not require me to train a model first. But without a basic idea of how a model processes input, it is difficult to reason about context length, document splitting, or answer quality.

I started again with tokens. Before text reaches a model, it is split into tokens and mapped to vectors. A token is not always a Chinese character, a word, or a sentence; the exact segmentation depends on the tokenizer.

## What self-attention does

Self-attention can be understood as each token looking at other tokens in the context while producing its current representation.

Each token is transformed into Q, K, and V. Q and K determine how strongly positions should attend to one another. After scaling and softmax, the resulting weights are used to combine the V vectors.

The formula is technical, but the practical question is simple: which parts of the context should the current content use more heavily?

## Why scale the scores

When Q and K have large dimensions, their dot products can become large. Applying softmax directly can make the weights too concentrated and the computation less stable. Attention therefore divides by `sqrt(d_k)` before normalization.

During inference, the model mainly performs forward computation and predicts the next token step by step. This does not mean that it updates its parameters while answering a user.

## Why this matters for applications

Understanding tokens and context helps explain why long documents are split, why prompts that are too long can affect context, why RAG retrieves relevant material before generation, and why the same question can produce different answers with different context organization.

I am not presenting this as model-training experience. My goal is to use these fundamentals when configuring AI applications, connecting knowledge bases, calling tools, and accepting product behavior.

For me, learning the model basics is useful because it clarifies what a Dify workflow, a DeepSeek API call, or a RAG pipeline is actually doing, and where its reliability boundaries are.
